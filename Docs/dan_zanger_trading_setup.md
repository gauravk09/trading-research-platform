# Dan Zanger — Trading Setup Reference

> **Dan Zanger** (momentum swing trader; former Beverly Hills pool contractor who turned ~$10,775 into ~$42 million in 23 months during the 1998–2000 dot-com run, setting a verified record for percentage portfolio gain in a single year. Publishes *The Zanger Report* newsletter at chartpattern.com.)

## Overview

Zanger is a momentum breakout swing trader. His edge is buying the **strongest stocks in the strongest groups** as they break out of well-formed bases on a **volume surge**, concentrating capital aggressively into the few that "rocket," and ruthlessly dumping anything that doesn't move immediately. He combines O'Neil-style CAN SLIM stock selection (he attended a William O'Neil seminar in the 1990s) with classic chart-pattern technical analysis. The method exploits the tendency of low-float, high-earnings-growth leaders to make explosive, trend-following moves once institutions start accumulating.

His own framing: he is a **systematic trader who applies discretion** — he buys the same types of stocks, from the same types of bases, with the same price action, continuously — but reads the daily bar + volume and the market environment to decide when to press and when to pass.

> **Important caveat:** Zanger's record run happened in an extraordinary market (the dot-com bubble) and he used **aggressive margin and concentration** that he himself does *not* recommend to inexperienced traders. He explicitly changed his methods after his account grew large. Treat the numbers below as his stated rules from that era, not a risk-managed retail template.

## Universal Rules (Apply to All Setups)

| Rule | Value |
|------|-------|
| Entry chase limit | **Never pay more than 5% above the trendline / breakout point** |
| Stop placement | ~**$1 below the breakout point**; more leeway for pricier stocks but **never more than a $2 stop loss** (his stated rule). Some traders use a 5% stop instead. |
| Partial sell | Sell **20–30% of the position** as the stock moves **15–20%** above the breakout point |
| Volume filter | Breakout volume must be **above the recent trend** — know the stock's **30-day average volume** |
| Margin | **Never use margin** until you've mastered the market, charts, and your emotions ("margin can wipe you out") |
| Typical holding period | 20 minutes to 10 weeks; **most trades 6–12 days** (varies by market) |

**Position sizing — the honest version:**
Zanger's *actual* sizing during his run was **pyramiding + aggressive concentration**, not a fixed-fraction model. He buys a **small starter position** on the breakout, and if the stock "really blasts off" he "backs up the truck" and adds heavily — "concentration of money in the big movers." Famously made heavy use of margin.

A commonly cited **"risk no more than 1% of the portfolio per trade"** rule appears in secondhand recaps but is **not** something Zanger states in his own primary interviews — treat it as a sanitized/CAN SLIM-influenced framing rather than his documented record-era behavior. Likewise, the **"8% stop loss"** rule circulating online is the classic O'Neil/CAN SLIM rule; Zanger's *own* stated stop is the "$1 below breakout, max $2, or 5%" rule above.

## Stock Selection (Prerequisites Before ANY Setup)

A stock must broadly meet these before Zanger considers a pattern entry:

- **Leading group / hot theme** — in a strong sector tied to a new technology or trend (his run: internet stocks). Follow strong groups; keep selections inside them.
- **Explosive earnings & sales surprise** — typically **earnings and revenue up 100%+**, *and* the figure must **surprise Wall Street** (the reaction to earnings matters more than the raw number).
- **Low float / low institutional sponsorship** — small share counts cause faster, larger price moves; he wants to be early before institutions pile in.
- **High-priced quality leader near 52-week highs** — he does **not** chase cheap stocks; he wants large run potential with manageable risk.
- **Well-formed base or pattern** — clean, identifiable structure (see patterns below). "Winning horses don't back up into the gate."

He scans **~1,400–1,500 charts once or twice a day** to find ~a couple dozen candidates.

## Setup 1 — Breakout from a Well-Formed Base (Core Setup)

**Type:** Momentum swing trade · Daily chart · His primary, continuously-traded setup

### Prerequisites (must meet ALL)
- Stock passes the stock-selection filters above (leading group, earnings surprise, low float, near highs)
- Has a **well-formed base or pattern** with a definable trendline / breakout point
- You know the stock's **30-day average volume** going in

### Entry Rule
- **Buy as the stock crosses above the trendline / breakout point of the base** — *with* a confirming **explosion of volume** (volume above the recent trend shortly after the breakout)
- **Never pay up more than 5% above the trendline.** If it's already extended past that, pass.
- For some low-volume names he'll **nibble just before** the breakout as he sees quiet accumulation — but warns this often goes against you, so it's discretionary, not the default.
- A buy point being "touched" is **not** an automatic buy — confirm with that day's volume and the overall market environment first.

### Stop Loss
- Stop ~**$1 below the breakout point** (the breakout point should now act as support).
- Pricier stocks can be given a little more room, but **never more than a $2 stop**.
- Alternative he acknowledges: a **5% stop** — which may mean selling a failed breakout within 20 minutes to a few hours of buying.
- **Be very quick to sell if it falls back under the trendline / breakout point.** "When the break occurs, I am a seller. No questions asked." He can always buy it back if it revalidates.

### The "20-Minute" Rule (his most distinctive exit)
- If the stock **doesn't accelerate sharply out of the base, sell it promptly that same day** — even after only 20 minutes, **regardless of profit or loss**. "If a stock isn't moving up sharply right away, then the trade must be wrong." Don't tie up capital in a stock going nowhere.

### Partial Exit — Locking In Profits
- **Sell 20–30% of the position** once the stock is up **15–20%** from the breakout point.
- Continuously sell strength: "I sell all or part of my position as it moves up strongly and then roll that money into new stocks coming out of fresh patterns."

### Riding the Winner / Full Exit
- **Hold your strongest stocks the longest.** Sell laggards quickly — "the only good stock is a stock going up."
- **He does NOT use trailing stops and does NOT use price targets.** He lets the stock's own action take him in and out.
- **Primary sell signal: a break of the support trendline.** When price breaks the up-trendline, he sells — no questions asked.
- As a move matures, he **learns to draw new, higher trendlines** and watches for **reversal patterns / candlestick signals** to time exits.
- He'll also peel back stocks that have gotten **too steep in their angle of ascent** when the broader market is extended.

### Expected Return
- No fixed target ("no one has a clue where a stock will ultimately go"). Big winners in his run ran **$30–$50+ points**. He accepts selling early and missing upside because "there is always another of my stocks close by that may advance $30–$50."

## Setup 2 — Pyramiding Into a Confirmed Winner (Add-On Tactic)

**Type:** Position-building tactic layered on top of Setup 1

### Trigger
- After a starter buy, if the stock **crosses the breakout point on an explosion of volume** and "rockets up," **add aggressively** — "back up the truck and get as much stock as I can, because I know we're going for a ride."

### Rule
- Start **small** (you never know in advance which breakouts will work), then **concentrate into the ones that prove themselves** on day one. "This is where the big returns come in: concentration of money in the big movers and selling stocks that fail to rocket out of their bases on the first day."

### Stop / Exit
- Same trendline-break and 20-minute logic as Setup 1, applied to the combined position.

## Setup 3 — Shorting / Going to Cash (Defensive, Late-Cycle)

**Type:** Used only when the market trend breaks · Not his bread-and-butter

### When
- After the market has run for a substantial period, leaders become **vulnerable to fast, hard sell-offs**.
- When breakouts **start failing across his watchlist**, that's the market signaling a correction.

### Action
- Start to **peel back the steepest extended stocks**, raise cash, and **prepare to add shorts as fast as possible once the trend is broken**.
- Add fresh longs only to names **just coming out of sound bases** while reducing extended ones (progressive exposure management).

### Caveat
- Zanger states the **biggest gains are on the long side** in leading stocks and that **shorting is generally tougher** — he is not a frequent short seller. Treat this as a hedging/defense tool, not a primary profit engine.

## Core Philosophy

- **"The only good stock is a stock going up."** Capital must always be working in the fastest mover; never marry a position that's stalled.
- **Volume is everything.** "It takes volume to move stocks." A breakout without a volume surge is suspect. Know each stock's 30-day average volume.
- **Trade leaders in leading groups only.** Pattern + price action + group strength together create the edge — not the pattern alone.
- **It's the *reaction* to earnings, not just the earnings.** He hunts positive earnings/revenue surprises in low-float names before institutions arrive.
- **Cut instantly, concentrate ruthlessly.** Sell failures (even in 20 minutes), pour money into the few that explode. "Winning horses don't back up into the gate."
- **No forecasts, no targets.** "I never say the market is going to go up or down… I allow the stock by its own actions to put me into the trade, and then by its actions to take me out."
- **Selling is the hardest skill** and took him longest to learn. The discipline to obey his trendlines is what protects the gains.
- **Put in the reps.** He scanned ~1,400–1,500 charts once or twice daily on 12-hour workdays. The edge is built on volume of practice and screen time.
- **Experience across all market types is mandatory** — you can't fully exploit the current environment until you've been hurt by, and learned from, every kind of market.
